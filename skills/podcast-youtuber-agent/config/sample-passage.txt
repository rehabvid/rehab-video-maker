Here's the thing I keep coming back to. In the last three years, the cost of
training a frontier model fell sharply, while the amount of genuinely useful
data did not double. That gap is where the interesting work begins. It is also
where most of the easy explanations start to break down.

Moritz Baier-Lentz makes a sharper version of the argument. He thinks language
is a lossy abstraction of reality. A transcript tells you what someone said. A
game tells you what they did, in space and time, with consequences. That sounds
narrow, until you ask what kind of data an agent actually needs. Reading about
a kitchen is not the same as opening a drawer, dropping a glass, and learning
that the floor is now wet.

So the question is not whether models can talk. They can. The question is
whether they can act. And if the best training ground is a game, what happens
when the game designs itself? That is the part I cannot answer yet. It is also
the reason I keep listening.
